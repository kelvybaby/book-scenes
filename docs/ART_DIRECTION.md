# Art direction

## Emotional thesis

The project should feel like entering the reader's emotional memory of a book rather than viewing an illustration of its plot. The world is lush, lyrical, graceful, and splendid, but never merely decorative. Beauty should carry waiting, distance, secrecy, weather, age, and contradiction.

The scene exists to give memorable language a physical home. A passage is not pasted over an image; it is discovered through an object whose meaning and behavior belong to the world.

## First world: Under the Almond Trees

The first tableau looks across the Park of the Evangels toward Fermina Daza's house. It is based on the early spatial relationship in the book without attempting an archaeological reconstruction.

The house is an emotionally truthful composite: recognizably one house and simultaneously a vessel for the atmosphere of the unnamed Caribbean city. Florentino is almost lost in the park's foreground shadow; Fermina is small and partially screened at the doorway beneath the almond trees. Neither becomes a portrait. The park, façade, courtyard, threshold, vegetation, telegraph wires, and diminishing light hold the charged distance between them.

## Three mood words

- **Lush** — abundant vegetation, surface, colour, air, and sensory detail
- **Suspended** — a place caught between arrival and departure, afternoon and night, speech and secrecy
- **Watchful** — framed views, partial figures, thresholds, distance, and attention crossing the square

## World metaphor

The city behaves like an instrument of correspondence.

- Telegraph wires carry attention as well as messages.
- Windows edit what can be seen.
- The park bench makes waiting architectural.
- The road out of the square becomes distance and eventually the journey.
- Flowers, paper, fabric, plaster, and shadow preserve traces of touch and time.

## Decay across the collection

Decay is a long-form visual grammar, not a single aesthetic filter. Social, ecological, bodily, moral, and mnemonic decay should appear only when the active scene and supplied text support them.

In the first scene, decay is materially young and specific: a half-ruined house, dry fountain, weeds in flowerpots, worn plaster, dead and yellow leaves, seasonal heat, and empty streets. Its counterforce is the bright, inhabited patio. Later scenes may deepen or transform this grammar rather than repeating the same ruined surfaces everywhere.

## Visual principles

1. **Scene before interface.** The visitor should first encounter a place, not a navigation system.
2. **Painterly specificity.** Surfaces and objects should feel observed and materially distinct, not covered by one nostalgic filter.
3. **Layered depth.** Depth comes from composition, atmosphere, occlusion, and restrained motion rather than constant 3D spectacle.
4. **Indirect figures.** Begin with silhouettes, hands, clothing, reflections, or distant figures unless consistent character studies justify more.
5. **Spatial language.** Quote reveals should inherit the material and behavior of their host object.
6. **Controlled abundance.** The scene may be lush, but each focal element must remain readable.
7. **Ambiguity over sentimentality.** Avoid reducing complicated longing to uncomplicated romance.

## Palette relationships

Exact values remain provisional until concept frames are compared.

- Warm, sun-struck limestone or fever yellow as the architectural field
- Oxidized botanical green in shutters, foliage, and shadow transitions
- Bruised bougainvillea, cochineal, or muted magenta as a concentrated living accent
- Nocturnal indigo for deepening sky and late shadow
- Paper ivory for passage surfaces
- Warm near-black or ink brown rather than neutral digital black

Use four to six dominant colours. Let light and material create variation instead of adding unrelated accents.

## Typography voices

Typography must be selected after the visual treatment, not before it.

- **Passage voice:** a literary serif with excellent long-form readability, expressive italics, and enough historical character without becoming costume typography
- **Utility voice:** a restrained sans-serif or occasional mono for quiet controls, discovery counts, and scene navigation
- **Optional handwritten trace:** limited to a genuine marginal or personal function; never used for full passages

Passages receive generous line height, controlled measure, and a stable reading surface. Animated text must retain a complete accessible name and remain readable without animation.

## Material language

- Limewashed or weathered plaster
- Dark wood and painted shutters
- Woven or embroidered fabric
- Waxed, folded, or pressed paper
- Almond leaves, petals, bark, dust, and dry-season heat
- Metal telegraph wire and small fittings
- Soft wear caused by weather and use, not generic grunge

Paper texture belongs to passage surfaces and selected transitions, not as a filter covering the entire world.

## Composition and camera

- The camera observes from elsewhere in the park rather than borrowing Florentino's point of view.
- Florentino and the bench form a small, consciously visible silhouette in the left or left-middle foreground/midground; no close head, shoulder, or over-the-shoulder framing.
- Fermina remains slightly more legible at the doorway through posture, clothing, and light. Her small downcast profile should resolve cleanly when inspected without becoming a focal portrait.
- Almond vegetation frames the park and also grows substantially at the house doorway, creating a sheltered threshold around Fermina.
- The house façade is readable but not perfectly frontal or symmetrical.
- One doorway, courtyard opening, or window carries the principal architectural focus.
- The composition preserves calm negative areas for passage reveals without making them look like marketing copy zones.

## Light

The initial lighting idea moves from bright late afternoon toward blue evening. Tree shade retains warm colour, air, and foliage detail rather than collapsing into near-black. Any change should be almost imperceptible: lengthening shadow, warming lamps, cooling distance, gathering insects, and increasing separation between paper ivory and nocturnal surroundings.

This progression is provisional. A strong static time of day is preferable to a weak or distracting cycle.

## Motion grammar

Motion is caused, localized, and slow.

- Leaves and fabric respond to wind.
- Depth layers respond subtly to viewer movement.
- Wire trembles after contact.
- Paper folds and unfolds.
- Petals shift or release under touch.
- Light changes with time or a visible source.
- Insects gather around illumination.

Ambient motion must stop when the scene is hidden. Pointer parallax should be measured in small offsets, not sweeping camera travel. Reduced motion replaces movement with stable visual states.

## Interaction grammar

- Discovery begins with a restrained material cue: a small shift, sound, light change, or cursor/focus response.
- Hover may invite, but click, tap, keyboard focus, and activation must complete the interaction.
- The quotation reveal stays spatially related to its object on large screens.
- Mobile may use a composed bottom sheet when an anchored label would become cramped.
- Closing a passage returns the scene to the exact prior state.
- No scores, collectibles language, achievement effects, or mandatory discovery order.

## Sound

Sound is optional and user-initiated. Potential elements include insects, leaves, distant city resonance, wood, paper, and barely perceptible environmental tone. Avoid trailer music, sentimental scoring, autoplay, and constant foreground sound.

## Explicit exclusions

- Generic sepia magical-realism collage
- A direct imitation of another artist or award site
- Glossy AI HDR, plastic surfaces, or hyper-detailed faces
- Glass navigation pills and startup-dashboard components
- Aurora gradients, floating blobs, laser effects, and decorative particle fields
- Constant split-text, scramble-text, or word-by-word animation
- Excessive bloom, blur, chromatic aberration, or film damage
- Butterflies or yellow flowers used as automatic shorthand without scene-specific justification
- Ornamental 3D that competes with the illustration
- Faux-old typography that makes passages difficult to read

## Future books

The project may share interaction ethics, accessibility, and passage behavior across books. It should not force every book into this palette, architecture, or material language. Each book earns its own world metaphor and visual constitution.
